# PART A — ECONOMY REBALANCING

## 2. Mining must matter

Currently mining is not important enough.

The current balance includes approximately:

- Homeworld: 240 Alloy/min passive
- Frontier: 55 Alloy/min passive
- Middle: 90 Alloy/min passive
- Core: 100 Alloy/min passive
- Mining: 200 Alloy per 40-second cycle

This can produce enough passive Alloy to support substantial shipbuilding without meaningful mining.

We want to change this.

### Design objective

**Players should be able to start playing and build a few ships without mining, but sustained military expansion should require active asteroid extraction and control of resource-producing territory.**

A player who never mines should eventually be limited by Alloy.

A player who secures mining operations should be able to:

- rebuild losses faster
- afford heavier ships earlier
- invest in planetary defenses
- maintain military pressure

Mining should be a genuine strategic advantage.

However, do not make it impossible for a defeated player to recover.

Protected homeworld income must still support modest rebuilding.

### Required work

Review and rebalance:

- homeworld passive income
- frontier income
- middle-system income
- core income
- asteroid mining output
- mining cycle durations
- ship costs
- defensive structure costs
- income bonuses
- pirate salvage
- recovery assistance

Keep Credits, Alloy and Fuel as our only three resources.

Do not create additional resources.

### Economy target

For an actively expanding commander, aim for mining to provide a meaningful proportion of their available shipbuilding Alloy.

An initial testing target could be approximately **30–50% of acquired Alloy coming from extraction** for commanders actively pursuing mining.

This is a tuning target, not a guaranteed percentage for every strategy.

Passive production should support survival and basic activity.

Mining should support sustained military competitiveness.

Do not force everyone into exactly the same mining strategy.

## 3. Improve mining interaction

We do not want to solve the mining problem by creating excessive clicking.

The game is fast-paced but not meant to be an APM-intensive RTS.

Review the current mining interaction.

Currently, a mining expedition completes one cycle and becomes idle.

Consider implementing:

**Automatic repeat mining**

When issuing a mining command, the player can choose:

- Mine once
- Continue mining

Continue Mining should automatically repeat extraction while:

- the fleet remains at the asteroid field
- the owner still controls the system
- the fleet is not in combat
- mining is legal
- applicable capacity/rules permit it

The player should be able to cancel the order.

Mining should stop or suspend appropriately when attacked.

Show:

- active mining fleet
- extraction progress
- resource yield
- cycle duration
- ongoing mining status

If unlimited mining creates an economic exploit, introduce a simple bounded mechanism such as extraction capacity, diminishing returns, or finite reserves with limited regeneration.

Do not add a complicated depletion economy unless testing demonstrates it is needed.

### Important

Players should have reasons to fight over valuable asteroid fields.

Not all fields need to produce exactly the same amount.

Introduce modest variation in:

- richness
- extraction value
- location
- strategic importance

The galaxy generator should distribute those opportunities fairly.

---

## 4. Rebalance Fuel

Previous simulations showed that Fuel tends to accumulate in large quantities while Alloy remains restrictive.

Investigate whether Fuel has sufficient economic purpose.

Review:

- movement costs
- mining rewards
- passive generation
- fleet size versus Fuel consumption
- retreat costs
- long-distance expeditions

Fuel should matter when committing fleets to distant operations, but it should not constantly prevent players from participating.

Avoid a situation where a commander has powerful fleets but cannot perform any meaningful action because they are permanently Fuel-starved.

Prefer understandable operating costs over additional resource sinks that exist solely to remove excess currency.

---

## 5. Review ship progression

Review the four existing classes:

- Frigate
- Destroyer
- Cruiser
- Dreadnought

Assess whether:

- Frigates are too cost-effective.
- Destroyers have a clear purpose.
- Cruisers justify their cost.
- Dreadnoughts are achievable within a 40-minute match.
- Players naturally diversify fleets.
- Bots understand when to save for stronger ships.
- Early heavy-ship rushes become too powerful.
- Losing expensive ships creates meaningful consequences.

Do not radically redesign the entire combat system.

Use the existing local combat resolver.

Make justified balance adjustments and test them.

## 7. Defense model

Space Wars currently has star systems containing multiple planets.

Ownership is handled at the STAR SYSTEM level.

We are not implementing individual planet conquest in this update.

However, defensive structures should belong to individual planets.

Each owned planet can support:

**One Orbital Defense Station**

and

**One Planetary Railgun Battery**

Both can exist simultaneously.

Each structure has its own upgrade level.

Initial maximum:

**Level 3**

A planet with no defensive structures can build either or both.

Do not allow unlimited stacking of identical structures on one planet.

### Important balance restriction

Because systems contain multiple planets, a system may eventually contain several defensive installations.

Do not make defensive strength scale so aggressively that a three-planet system with upgraded structures becomes practically unconquerable.

Introduce sensible contribution limits or escalating costs where necessary.

Use the simulator to determine reasonable values.

---

## 8. Orbital Defense Station

This should be the main general-purpose defensive installation.

Suggested role:

- substantial durability
- balanced defensive weapons
- efficient against small and medium ships
- some point-defense/interception capability if the existing resolver supports an appropriate simplified mechanic
- helps protect the system when the owner has no fleet present

The station should participate automatically when an enemy attacks its star system.

It is stationary.

It cannot be assigned to fleets.

It cannot travel.

### Upgrade levels

Level 1:

Basic orbital protection.

Level 2:

Improved durability and firepower.

Level 3:

Powerful defensive station.

Higher levels should provide visible improvements without becoming disproportionately efficient compared with building ships.

---

## 9. Planetary Railgun Battery

This should be a different defensive specialization.

Suggested role:

- powerful long-range anti-ship weapon
- especially useful against Cruisers and Dreadnoughts
- slower firing rate
- weaker against swarms of small ships
- less durable than an orbital station
- substantial damage when left uncontested

The railgun should provide a meaningful reason to mix fleets.

For example, attacking a heavily fortified system with only Dreadnoughts should not always be optimal.

Frigates and Destroyers should retain some tactical usefulness.

### Upgrade levels

Level 1:

Basic planetary anti-ship battery.

Level 2:

Improved damage and targeting.

Level 3:

Heavy strategic railgun capable of seriously threatening capital ships.

Do not give it absurd one-shot damage that makes siege impossible.

---

## 10. Starting homeworld defenses

Every commander begins with a protected homeworld.

At the beginning of every match, automatically place:

**Orbital Defense Station — Level 1**

**Planetary Railgun Battery — Level 1**

on that commander's primary home planet.

These starting structures are free and included in the standard starting package.

All eight commanders receive identical starting defensive capabilities.

Home systems remain non-capturable and non-attackable under the current prototype rules.

The defenses should still be visible so players understand the system and can inspect them.

Do not mistakenly use homeworld defenses to permit attacks on otherwise protected homes.

If future homeworld raids are introduced, these structures can become functional there.

For now, the free home defenses mainly establish the feature and provide a consistent starting state.

## 12. Construction times and costs

Defenses are a strategic investment.

They must consume resources.

Use:

- Credits
- Alloy

Fuel generally should not be required to construct a stationary defense.

Suggested prototype philosophy:

Level 1:

Affordable in the early-to-mid game.

Level 2:

Meaningful investment.

Level 3:

Expensive and most appropriate for strategic/core/frontier strongholds.

Construction should take time.

Do not allow instantaneous defensive structures to appear during an active battle.

Examples of starting timing ranges for simulation:

Orbital Station Level 1:
45–75 seconds

Orbital Station Level 2:
75–110 seconds

Orbital Station Level 3:
110–160 seconds

Railgun Level 1:
35–60 seconds

Railgun Level 2:
60–100 seconds

Railgun Level 3:
100–150 seconds

These are initial tuning ranges.

Choose final prototype values based on economic simulation.

The system should have bounded construction capacity so players cannot instantly upgrade every structure everywhere.

Avoid making defensive construction so expensive that no bot/player ever uses it.

## 13. Defense in combat

Defenses must participate in actual battles.

Do not implement them as decorative defense-power numbers that never affect the outcome.

When an enemy fleet attacks a fortified system:

Defending forces should include:

- stationed defending fleets
- operational orbital stations
- operational railgun batteries

Use the existing local combat tick system or a compatible extension.

### Combat behavior

Orbital Stations:

- use their own hull and damage characteristics
- attack eligible enemies
- can be targeted and destroyed

Railgun Batteries:

- fire at suitable enemy ships
- have a slower firing cadence
- favor larger targets
- can be targeted and destroyed

Attackers should be able to target or destroy defenses.

Focus-fire and stance interactions should remain sensible.

Defensive structures should not count toward mobile fleet limits.

### Capture

An attacker cannot capture a system while active hostile defenses remain operational.

Winning the space battle should allow the attacker to clear remaining defenses through the same combat system.

Only after defenders have been overcome can the normal capture timer begin.

Do not create an unwinnable stalemate where the attacker has defeated the fleet but cannot interact with a remaining station.

---

## 14. Damage, destruction and repair

Defensive installations should have a meaningful lifecycle.

Possible states:

- Not built
- Constructing
- Operational
- Damaged
- Destroyed
- Repairing
- Upgrading

If a defense survives an attack with damage, it should remain damaged.

It should not instantly heal fully.

Allow repair using a modest resource cost and construction time.

Prevent upgrades/repairs from instantly completing during battle.

Destroyed installations should need reconstruction or a justified repair rule.

### Ownership changes

When a system is captured, its previous owner's defenses must not automatically become free fully operational defenses for the attacker.

For this prototype, prefer destroyed/disabled hostile defenses becoming ruins or empty slots after capture, with any salvage carefully bounded.

Clearly define this rule.

No duplicate rewards or exploitable ownership-flip rebuilding.

# PART C — BOT STRATEGY

## 16. Teach bots to mine properly

Bots currently appear to undervalue mining.

Investigate their existing utility logic.

Bots must understand that Alloy is necessary for sustained military production.

Their mining decisions should account for:

- current Alloy reserves
- expected production spending
- pending ship construction
- nearby asteroid opportunities
- mining output
- travel time
- enemy danger
- alternative expansion opportunities

An Industrialist should mine more often.

However, other personalities should still mine when their economies require it.

Do not simply force each bot to mine on a fixed timer.

Mining should be a rational economic decision.

If automatic mining is implemented, bots should use it appropriately.

---

## 17. Teach bots to build defenses

Bots must be able to:

- build orbital stations
- build railgun batteries
- upgrade structures
- repair damaged structures
- choose appropriate planets/systems
- avoid over-investing

Personality should matter.

### Turtle

Strong preference for defending valuable systems.

### Industrialist

Defends valuable mining sites and resource infrastructure.

### Expansionist

Builds modest defenses to secure newly conquered territory.

### Aggressor

Spends more on fleets, fewer defensive investments.

### Opportunist

Fortifies useful objectives and chokepoints.

These are tendencies, not absolute rules.

A bot should not build defenses on a system it expects to lose immediately.

A bot should not construct maximum-level defenses on every planet while neglecting fleet production.

---

## 18. Strategic positioning

The AI should understand the difference between:

- safe interior systems
- exposed border systems
- strategic systems
- resource systems
- major objectives

Defenses should matter more at:

- contested borders
- chokepoints
- valuable asteroid systems
- Core systems
- strategic bonus systems

Bots should recognize difficult fortified targets and adjust their strategy.

Possible responses:

- bring larger fleet
- attack elsewhere
- reinforce
- delay attack until stronger
- retreat when losing

Avoid repeatedly sacrificing tiny fleets against a powerful railgun battery.

## 20. Balance objectives

Try to achieve the following:

**Mining matters.**

A commander that completely ignores mining should usually have weaker sustained production than an otherwise comparable commander that mines effectively.

**Defenses matter.**

A defended border system should require a meaningful additional attacking commitment.

**Defenses are not unbeatable.**

Players should be able to defeat fortified systems using appropriate fleets.

**Expansion remains useful.**

We should not accidentally turn the optimal strategy into sitting at home and building stations.

**Ship classes matter.**

No single ship class should dominate every economic and combat situation.

**Bots behave intelligently enough.**

Bots should build, mine, attack and defend without obvious self-destructive loops.

**Comebacks remain possible.**

A weakened commander should be able to rebuild modest forces and contest territory.

**The match remains approximately 40 minutes.**

Do not substantially slow the overall game because defenses exist.

**Strategic objectives remain attractive.**

Pirates, Guardian, Leviathan and strategic sites should retain meaningful risk/reward.
